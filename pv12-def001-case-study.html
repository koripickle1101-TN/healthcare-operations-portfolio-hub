Where Did the Workflow
First Lose Control?

The problem was not a denied claim. It was a fictional administrative checkpoint that could label an incomplete authorization decision ready. This case study traces how that false-ready branch was detected, repaired, and retested.

THE QA STORY

One gap. A safer exception path.

01 / DETECT

Identify the false-ready condition

The original formula did not explicitly handle a blank or unrecognized value in PV FIXTURES!E2, the authorization-applicability input. A negative test showed such a value could produce None detected and Ready for human QA. This was an internally simulated control failure, not proof of a real adverse event.

02 / DIAGNOSE

Locate the first failing gate

The worksheet evaluates checkpoints in order: registration, eligibility documentation, authorization applicability/status, task ownership, handoff destination, and escalation. The control loss was a missing fallback branch in the applicability logic—not evidence that an insurer authorized or denied care.

03 / PREVENT

Require an explicit applicability state

A disposable workbook was updated to recognize the defined states Required, Undetermined, and Not Triggered. Invalid or missing values now generate Authorization applicability and Review. An Excel dropdown was also configured, although its Android UI rejection behavior remains unverified.

04 / IMPROVE & VALIDATE

Retest without rewriting the evidence

Sixteen existing HQA checks and three targeted negative checks passed in separate file-based regression testing: 19 of 19 within that defined test scope. Historical workbook QA entries were retained but were not all rerun. A further proposed control would require explicit handoff acknowledgment and human sign-off; it was not implemented.

Limits that matter

“Ready for human QA” means a fictional rule check is complete enough to send to a person. It does not mean cleared for care, payer approval, or that any human approval occurred. PV FIXTURES!Q2:Q13 records human approval as Not Performed. This is a spreadsheet demonstration, not a validated healthcare software product.
