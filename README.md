I built this hub to bring together the simulated projects I use to study one question: **where does a healthcare workflow first lose control before the problem reaches the patient, the staff member trying to fix it, or the revenue cycle?**

As I work toward my Bachelor's of Science degree in Healthcare Administration at the University of Phoenix, I am especially interested in patient access, eligibility verification, prior authorization, documentation readiness, denial prevention, and the handoffs that connect those areas.

My perspective is patient-to-professional. From the patient side, these processes do not feel like separate departments. A missed verification step, unclear requirement, documentation gap, or handoff failure can show up later as another phone call, a delayed appointment, a reschedule, billing confusion, or a denial. These projects help me practice tracing those problems back to the point where the workflow first became unstable.

## Why I Built This Portfolio

I do not have formal healthcare operations employment experience yet, so I use simulated projects to turn what I am learning into visible work.

Each project gives me a way to practice a different part of healthcare operations: organizing workflow logic, identifying risk, building simple scoring or review methods, designing dashboards, documenting assumptions, and explaining what an operational problem could mean downstream.

The goal is not to present these projects as production systems. The goal is to show how I am learning to think through healthcare workflow problems carefully, honestly, and in a way that stays connected to the patient experience.

## The Workflow Story

```text
Patient Access Bottleneck Intelligence System™ → Eligibility Verification → Prior Authorization → Denial Prevention
                    ↓                               ↓                    ↓                    ↓
         Registration / intake control loss   Patient access risk   Documentation risk   Claim/revenue risk

SBI asks where the first control loss occurred.
Habit Audit asks which recurring operational habits may be making that risk more likely.
```

What interests me most is the connection between steps. A front-end issue does not always stay at the front end. It can move downstream through authorization, documentation, claims, billing, and patient communication. My projects are built around finding that connection rather than treating every problem as an isolated task.

## What This Portfolio Is — and Is Not

This is a **student-developed educational portfolio**.

- All scenarios and data are simulated or synthetic.
- No protected health information (PHI) is used.
- No real patient, payer, claim, employer, or healthcare organization data is represented.
- These projects do not represent formal healthcare employment, production deployment, clinical decision-making, or payer decision authority.
- I do not claim that these projects have produced real-world cost savings, denial reductions, productivity gains, or patient outcomes.

I include those boundaries because credibility matters to me. I want the work to show what I can analyze and build at this stage without overstating what I have done professionally.

## How to Review the Portfolio

A useful review path is:

1. Start with the **Patient Access Bottleneck Intelligence System™** (Excel workbook) to see how a registration inaccuracy can cascade through eligibility, authorization readiness, and financial clearance.
2. Move to **EVIS** for eligibility and front-end patient access risk in more detail.
3. Continue to **PARCS** for prior authorization workflow risk, ownership, and escalation logic.
4. Continue to **DPIS** for upstream denial prevention and claim-readiness thinking.
5. Review **SBI** for cross-workflow breakpoint analysis.
6. Finish with the **Healthcare Operations Habit Audit** for a broader look at recurring operational habits, controls, and improvement priorities.
